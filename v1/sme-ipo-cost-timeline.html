Ranges assume a ₹10–50 Cr issue with financials that do not need major restatement. Underwriting commission, if applicable, is additional. Your own estimate is prepared after the readiness assessment.

Issue size

Several heads are fixed rather than proportional, so a ₹12 Cr issue carries a higher percentage cost than a ₹45 Cr one. Bigger issues dilute the fixed costs.

State of your financials

Restatement effort is the single biggest swing factor. Clean, audited, related-party-light books cost less to take through due diligence — and cause fewer exchange queries.

Sector and structure

Regulated sectors, group structures, overseas subsidiaries or pending litigation add diligence work on both the legal and audit side.

Total costs typically fall in a band of 7–10% of the issue size for a ₹10–50 Cr issue, spread across the merchant banker, legal counsel, auditor, RTA, exchange and ROC fees, market making and marketing. Smaller issues sit at the higher end of the band because several heads are fixed rather than proportional.

Costs are staged, not paid upfront. Auditor and legal fees fall during due diligence and drafting; the merchant banker is usually paid in tranches against milestones; exchange, ROC and RTA fees fall at filing and allotment; market-making costs run for three years after listing. We set the staging out in the consolidated estimate so you can plan cash flow.

Issue expenses are usually disclosed in the prospectus as one of the objects of the issue, so a large part is met from the proceeds. The expenses incurred before the issue opens — audit, legal, restructuring — are funded by the company in the meantime. We flag which heads fall on which side in your estimate.

It depends on how quickly restated financials, due diligence and exchange approvals move. You get a dated, step-by-step plan after the readiness assessment, and we front-load the two steps that usually cause delays: restated financials and exchange queries.

Three things, in our experience: restatement issues discovered late, related-party or litigation disclosures that need untangling, and a second round of exchange queries caused by a thin DRHP. All three are visible during the readiness assessment, which is why we insist on doing it first.
